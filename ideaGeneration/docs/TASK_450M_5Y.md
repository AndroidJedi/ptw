# Mission — Build a Company That Could Be Sold for $450M Within 5 Years

**Mission code:** `MISSION_450M_5Y`  
**Horizon:** approximately 5 years  
**Target outcome:** create a company with a plausible path to an acquisition / sale price of approximately **USD 450 million**.

This is an optimization target, not a guarantee.

---

## 1. Founder situation

The founder will be serving in the army and expects limited, irregular availability.

Therefore ideas that require continuous founder presence, daily operational management, constant manual sales, physical logistics, or a large founder-dependent organization are structurally weaker for this mission.

The company should increasingly operate through:

- software;
- automation;
- AI where it creates real leverage;
- self-serve workflows;
- small high-leverage teams;
- repeatable processes;
- delegated operations.

The founder must be able to supervise the company asynchronously and intervene mainly in high-value decisions.

---

## 2. What kinds of businesses are allowed

IT/software products are preferred because they fit the operating constraints, but do not artificially restrict the search to a conventional SaaS application.

The system may propose any legal and ethical business model if it can plausibly satisfy the mission and be operated with low founder involvement.

Examples of allowed directions include:

- consumer software;
- B2B software;
- AI-native products;
- platforms;
- marketplaces with highly automated operations;
- data products;
- developer infrastructure;
- fintech/software layers where regulatory burden is manageable;
- media/software hybrids;
- transaction businesses;
- software-enabled services that can rapidly become automated/productized;
- new categories not captured above.

Do not choose a category merely because it is fashionable.

---

## 3. Hard constraints

Every idea must explicitly address:

1. **Founder availability**  
   How can this operate if the founder has limited daily involvement?

2. **Scale**  
   Why can this become large enough that a ~$450M exit is at least economically plausible?

3. **Five-year path**  
   What could make a strategic buyer or financial buyer pay roughly that amount within about five years?

4. **Distribution**  
   How can customers/users be acquired without relying indefinitely on founder-led selling?

5. **Automation / leverage**  
   Which parts of delivery, support, operations, growth, or decision-making become software-driven?

6. **Defensibility**  
   If the concept works, what becomes harder to copy over time?

7. **Validation**  
   What is the smallest real-world test that could quickly disprove the key assumption?

---

## 4. Preferences, not absolute rules

Prefer ideas with:

- global or very large addressable markets;
- high gross margins;
- low marginal cost;
- recurring or repeatable revenue;
- network, data, workflow, integration, brand, ecosystem, or distribution advantages;
- a path to a small high-output team;
- fast initial validation;
- the ability to automate founder responsibilities;
- a clear strategic-acquirer story.

Penalize, but do not automatically forbid:

- capital-intensive businesses;
- businesses needing large field operations;
- custom consulting disguised as software;
- businesses where every sale requires the founder;
- businesses with weak distribution logic;
- ideas depending on one speculative technical breakthrough;
- extreme regulatory dependence;
- businesses whose only $450M argument is "the market is huge."

---

## 5. Required idea format

Every generated idea must contain:

### Title

Short and distinctive.

### One-liner

One sentence explaining what the company is.

### Customer

Who pays or creates the economic value?

### Problem

What painful or valuable problem exists?

### Product

What exactly is built?

### Business model

How does the company make money?

### Distribution

How can it acquire customers/users at scale?

### Automation

Why can it run with limited founder involvement?

### Five-year exit logic

A concrete argument for why this might plausibly become a ~$450M acquisition target within five years.

This must describe economic or strategic logic, not simply assert a valuation.

### Key risks

The most important reasons the idea could fail.

### First validation test

A small, concrete test that can invalidate the central assumption quickly.

---

## 6. Evaluation rubric — 100 points

Each reasoning context evaluates every idea using the same weighted rubric.

### A. Exit potential — 25 points

Question:

> Is there a credible route to a business that could be strategically or financially worth about $450M within approximately five years?

Look for:

- enough revenue/growth potential;
- strategic acquisition logic;
- category importance;
- buyer universe;
- compounding advantage.

### B. Founder independence — 20 points

Question:

> Can this become operationally independent of a founder with limited availability?

High score requires a credible route to automation, delegation, self-service, or a small autonomous team.

### C. Distribution — 15 points

Question:

> Is there a scalable and believable customer/user acquisition mechanism?

Penalize "we will run ads" or "we will hire salespeople" when no deeper distribution advantage exists.

### D. Scalability and economics — 15 points

Question:

> Can revenue/value scale substantially faster than operational complexity and cost?

### E. Defensibility — 15 points

Question:

> If successful, what compounds and becomes difficult to reproduce?

### F. Speed and capital efficiency — 10 points

Question:

> Can the core assumption be validated quickly and can the company reach meaningful traction without requiring enormous capital before learning?

Total:

```text
25 + 20 + 15 + 15 + 15 + 10 = 100
```

---

## 7. Evaluation philosophy

A fascinating idea is not automatically a good mission candidate.

A boring idea with excellent distribution, automation, economics, and acquisition logic may outrank a more exciting invention.

Evaluators must specifically challenge:

- hidden operational load;
- founder dependence;
- unrealistic distribution;
- fake network effects;
- weak willingness to pay;
- shallow AI wrappers;
- regulatory traps;
- valuation narratives unsupported by economics.

At the same time, evaluators must not punish genuinely unconventional ideas merely because they do not resemble existing SaaS companies.

The 30% explore share exists specifically to keep unconventional search alive.

---

## 8. Desired evolutionary behavior

Over generations, the system should ideally discover:

- stronger distribution mechanisms;
- less founder dependence;
- clearer monetization;
- better strategic buyer logic;
- more defensibility;
- faster validation paths;
- combinations of strong mechanisms found in different ideas;
- occasional entirely new branches.

Do not optimize for superficial wording improvements.

A new generation is better only if the underlying business candidate is materially better.

---

## 9. Owner interaction

Owner feedback is authoritative mission guidance.

Examples:

```text
"I cannot personally do outbound sales."
"Keep the core of idea #31 but remove physical logistics."
"I am willing to raise capital if the founder workload remains low."
"Do not assume I can spend every day managing employees."
```

Future EVOLVE cycles must include active owner guidance.

Historical generations and scores remain unchanged.
