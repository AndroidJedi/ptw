# Marketing approaches — policy version 1

The server supplies exactly one selected policy snapshot. Its approach is an
owner preference for this hypothesis, never proof or a universal learned rule.
Keep the raw idea's capabilities and the existing honest offer/CTA boundaries.
Return positioning in the required language: desired_identity (empty when not
useful), customer_tension, category_frame, and functional_value. These explain
one decision, not alternative campaigns. Keep the whole positioning object,
including marketing_approach, below 1024 UTF-8 bytes; prefer short sentences.
No invented capabilities, health claims, premium prices, status stereotypes,
scarcity, customer proof, or manufactured anxiety. Brand examples below explain
mechanics from an owner-supplied transcript; they are not performance evidence.

## benefit_led

Lead with the customer's concrete problem, practical outcome and supported
benefits. Choose one differentiation using the existing direct-response method.
Keep the offer low-friction and honor-able. Positioning explains the customer's
tension, the ordinary useful category and the practical reason to choose this
product. An emotional benefit may support that practical promise; desired_identity
may be empty. Do not force a lifestyle or premium-category story.

## identity_led

Choose one primary mechanic below that fits this idea; use a supporting mechanic
only when it sharpens the same promise. Translate it into a specific customer's
desired identity, tension, meaningful category frame and real functional value.
If no expansive identity fits, use modest competence, clarity or ease grounded
in the task. Do not add a separate reasoning essay or a mechanic checklist.

- Touchland/Jolie, category reframing: when actual capabilities serve a broader
  customer goal, position the product in that context. A task planner can mean
  feeling prepared for the day. Packaging or health benefits absent from the
  idea cannot be invented to make the reframing work.
- Gathre, buyer tension: distinguish buyer from user when relevant. Resolve two
  legitimate desires, such as caring for a child and enjoying one's home.
  Do not infer a buyer's gender, wealth or taste from a stereotype.
- Graza, competence through use: make a supported task easier to understand
  through recognizable occasions and action language. A water-label comparison
  app can support informed shopping; it cannot diagnose water safety.
- Loop/Stanley, everyday identity: select a concrete use situation and an
  achievable self-image such as prepared, considerate, organized or confident.
  Show a believable interaction rather than generic luxury or group status.
- Starface/Welly, emotional reframing: where the idea establishes embarrassment
  or inconvenience, offer acceptance, expression or ease. Never create shame
  or anxiety so the product can appear to cure it.
- Liquid Death, category convention: name a frustrating convention such as
  jargon or tedious routines and express an honest alternative. A common enemy
  is a convention, not a fabricated danger or an attacked customer. Provocation,
  rebellion and community are optional, not universal requirements.
- Heyday, emotional appeal with a practical anchor: pair the desired feeling
  with a capability supplied by the idea. Convenience can support feeling able
  to care for oneself on a busy day; emotional language cannot replace function.

Example: for an app comparing bottled-water labels, use informed everyday choice
as identity, confusing labels as tension, a shopping helper as category, and
label comparison as functional value. A Post may say 'Know what you choose'; a
Landing can explain comparison and the approved offer. Never promise healthier
water, medical outcomes or unavailable analysis.
