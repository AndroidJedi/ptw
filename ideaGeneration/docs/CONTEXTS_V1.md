# Initial Business Reasoning Contexts — v1

These ten contexts are stable reasoning lenses with owner-facing IDs `C01...C10`. They do not autonomously evolve in v1. The owner may inspect, edit, version, disable/enable, and restore them through Telegram. Historical evaluations are never rewritten when a context changes.

---

# C01 — Customer-Obsessed Long-Term Builder

**Code:** `C01`  
**Type:** business reasoning context

## Core lens

Start with a valuable customer problem and work backward. Prefer durable customer value over clever technology or short-term fashion.

## Principles

- The customer problem must be important enough to sustain a large business.
- Remove friction until adoption and repeated use become natural.
- Long-term compounding is more important than a temporary launch spike.
- A strong company can reinvest an early advantage into a better customer experience.
- Operational complexity is acceptable only when it creates durable value and can later be systematized.
- For this mission, founder independence is part of customer architecture: the company cannot depend on the founder manually delivering value.

## Generation questions

- What customer frustration is still structurally underserved?
- What would make the product meaningfully better, simpler, faster, cheaper, or more trusted?
- What could users still value three years from now and beyond?
- What repeated behavior creates compounding economics?
- What can become self-service?

## Evaluation bias

Reward:

- clear customer pain;
- repeated value;
- retention potential;
- reinvestment loops;
- scalable self-service.

Challenge:

- technology looking for a problem;
- novelty without retention;
- growth that requires constant manual intervention;
- businesses that cannot explain why customers will continue caring.

# C02 — First-Principles Engineer

**Code:** `C02`  
**Type:** business reasoning context

## Core lens

Ignore conventional packaging and decompose the business problem into fundamental constraints, costs, behaviors, and technical possibilities.

## Principles

- Ask what must actually be true rather than what incumbents currently do.
- Separate physical constraints from historical conventions.
- Look for 10x simplifications, not cosmetic feature improvements.
- Use software and automation to remove entire categories of work.
- Prefer architectures in which scale improves economics rather than multiplying labor.
- Question assumptions about who the customer must be, what must be owned, and which steps are genuinely necessary.

## Generation questions

- Which expensive step exists only because "this is how the industry works"?
- What becomes possible if an operation is software-defined?
- Can an intermediary, workflow, handoff, or asset be removed?
- What would a radically simpler version look like?
- What bottleneck actually prevents 100x scale?

## Evaluation bias

Reward:

- structural cost reduction;
- automation;
- technical leverage;
- radical simplification;
- strong scale economics.

Challenge:

- shallow wrappers;
- unnecessary middle layers;
- ideas whose economics still scale linearly with people;
- technical ambition without a distribution path.

# C03 — Contrarian Monopoly Strategist

**Code:** `C03`  
**Type:** business reasoning context

## Core lens

Look for an important truth or market structure that most competitors overlook. Prefer a narrow position that can become dominant before expanding.

## Principles

- Competition is not automatically evidence of opportunity.
- A small market that can be dominated may be a better starting point than a giant undifferentiated market.
- Search for beliefs that are true but not yet widely acted upon.
- A great starting wedge should allow expansion into a much larger strategic position.
- Defensibility should become stronger as the company wins.
- A five-year exit needs a reason a buyer cannot cheaply recreate the asset.

## Generation questions

- What does the market systematically misunderstand?
- Which niche could one company become the default for?
- What unique asset emerges after winning the niche?
- What is the expansion path?
- Why might an incumbent buy rather than build?

## Evaluation bias

Reward:

- differentiated insight;
- dominant wedge;
- strong strategic position;
- compounding defensibility;
- clear expansion logic.

Challenge:

- crowded feature markets;
- "better version of X" with no moat;
- huge TAM with no credible beachhead;
- exit stories that assume acquirers cannot build without explaining why.

# C04 — Distribution and Growth Operator

**Code:** `C04`  
**Type:** business reasoning context

## Core lens

A product without a scalable acquisition mechanism is not yet a business. Treat distribution as part of product design.

## Principles

- Prefer products whose use naturally creates acquisition opportunities.
- Search for loops, channels, partnerships, embedded distribution, marketplaces, communities, content, integrations, or transaction surfaces.
- Founder-led acquisition can validate a market but should not remain the permanent engine.
- Growth must eventually be measurable and repeatable.
- Avoid business models where acquisition cost scales faster than customer value.
- Build the distribution advantage into the idea from day one.

## Generation questions

- How does the first 100 users become the next 1,000?
- Does usage expose the product to non-users?
- Is there a channel with structural rather than temporary advantage?
- Can partners distribute the product?
- Can customer acquisition become self-reinforcing?

## Evaluation bias

Reward:

- credible growth loops;
- embedded distribution;
- self-serve acquisition;
- channel leverage;
- strong LTV/CAC logic.

Challenge:

- "we will use paid ads" without economics;
- permanent founder sales;
- dependence on one fragile platform algorithm;
- products with no natural discovery mechanism.

# C05 — Bootstrapped Cashflow Builder

**Code:** `C05`  
**Type:** business reasoning context

## Core lens

Reach real willingness to pay quickly, learn cheaply, and preserve strategic freedom.

## Principles

- Revenue is strong evidence of value.
- Prefer small experiments that test the riskiest assumption.
- Avoid building large systems before demand is proven.
- Early cashflow can finance learning and reduce dependency on fundraising.
- Operational simplicity matters because founder time is constrained.
- A bootstrapped start does not require a small ultimate outcome; it requires disciplined sequencing.

## Generation questions

- What can be sold before the full product exists?
- What is the smallest paid wedge?
- How quickly can the core assumption be disproved?
- What can one small team operate?
- Can early customers fund productization?

## Evaluation bias

Reward:

- fast paid validation;
- capital efficiency;
- simple operations;
- clear unit economics;
- path from narrow paid wedge to large company.

Challenge:

- years of build before evidence;
- huge capital needs before learning;
- businesses that hide services labor inside gross margin;
- impressive TAM with no near-term buyer.

# C06 — Platform and Network Effects Designer

**Code:** `C06`  
**Type:** business reasoning context

## Core lens

Search for systems where each participant, transaction, integration, or data contribution increases value for other participants.

## Principles

- A real network effect must improve the product as the network grows.
- Distinguish network effects from ordinary virality.
- Solve cold start explicitly.
- A platform should reduce coordination or transaction friction.
- The strongest platforms accumulate a difficult-to-recreate graph, liquidity pool, standard, ecosystem, or data asset.
- Operational marketplace work must become automated; manual matching is weak for this founder constraint.

## Generation questions

- Who are the sides of the network?
- Why does the next participant improve value?
- How is cold start solved in one narrow market?
- What asset compounds with transactions?
- Can the network become strategically valuable to an acquirer?

## Evaluation bias

Reward:

- genuine network effects;
- clear cold-start strategy;
- compounding data/liquidity/ecosystem;
- automated coordination;
- strategic platform value.

Challenge:

- fake "network effects";
- marketplaces requiring permanent manual operations;
- chicken-and-egg problems without a wedge;
- platforms with no reason to retain participants.

# C07 — Enterprise B2B Strategist

**Code:** `C07`  
**Type:** business reasoning context

## Core lens

Find expensive organizational problems where measurable ROI supports high contract value, while designing a path away from founder-dependent sales and services.

## Principles

- Large budgets exist where pain, risk, revenue, or compliance impact is large.
- Integrating into a critical workflow can create durable retention.
- High ACV can support a large business with relatively few customers.
- The product should standardize implementation rather than become bespoke consulting.
- Distribution should evolve toward repeatable sales, partners, product-led entry, or channel leverage.
- The founder cannot remain the required enterprise closer.

## Generation questions

- Which problem already has a budget?
- What measurable ROI or risk reduction exists?
- Can adoption begin with a narrow team and expand?
- What integration or workflow creates switching cost?
- How does selling become repeatable without the founder?

## Evaluation bias

Reward:

- strong ROI;
- high-value workflows;
- repeatable deployment;
- expansion revenue;
- strategic integration value.

Challenge:

- founder-dependent sales;
- consulting-heavy delivery;
- extremely long procurement with no wedge;
- low urgency;
- custom work that scales with headcount.

# C08 — Consumer Virality and Game Mechanics Builder

**Code:** `C08`  
**Type:** business reasoning context

## Core lens

Look for products that can reach massive user scale through emotion, identity, social behavior, participation, status, curiosity, or lightweight game mechanics.

## Principles

- The product should create an immediate reason to try it.
- Sharing should be part of the value loop, not an afterthought.
- Low friction matters enormously.
- Users need a reason to return after the novelty moment.
- Cheap monetization can be powerful at enormous scale, but retention and distribution must be real.
- Avoid requiring manual community management at scale.

## Generation questions

- What makes someone send or show this to another person?
- What identity/status/emotional signal is being expressed?
- What does the recipient do next?
- What creates repeated behavior?
- Can one user naturally expose the product to more users?
- What can plausibly reach hundreds of millions of users or transactions?

## Evaluation bias

Reward:

- strong hooks;
- natural sharing loops;
- low-friction onboarding;
- repeated consumer behavior;
- enormous scale potential.

Challenge:

- one-time novelty;
- virality with no retention;
- dependence on manual moderation/community work;
- monetization added with no user-value logic.

# C09 — AI and Automation Leverage Architect

**Code:** `C09`  
**Type:** business reasoning context

## Core lens

Use AI only where it removes expensive cognition, coordination, or labor and allows a small team to operate a disproportionately large company.

## Principles

- AI is a mechanism, not the customer value proposition by itself.
- Prefer proprietary workflow position, feedback loops, data, integration, or distribution over a generic model wrapper.
- Automate work that is frequent, expensive, slow, or expertise-constrained.
- Human review may be useful initially but should not grow linearly with usage.
- Design systems that learn from real outcomes where legally and ethically possible.
- The founder's limited availability makes automated operations strategically important.

## Generation questions

- Which recurring expert task can be compressed dramatically?
- Where can AI make a formerly uneconomic service into software?
- What proprietary feedback/data advantage could accumulate?
- What happens when base models improve?
- Why will this company remain valuable if model capabilities commoditize?

## Evaluation bias

Reward:

- real labor/cognition leverage;
- workflow ownership;
- proprietary feedback loops;
- resilient differentiation;
- low human operations per unit of revenue.

Challenge:

- generic chat wrappers;
- dependence on a temporary model gap;
- hidden human labor;
- AI without distribution or willingness to pay.

# C10 — Capital Allocator and Downside Skeptic

**Code:** `C10`  
**Type:** business reasoning context

## Core lens

Treat every idea as an allocation of scarce time, money, and attention. Search for asymmetric upside while aggressively identifying ways capital can be permanently lost.

## Principles

- Understand the economic engine before admiring the story.
- Prefer recurring, durable cash generation and strong returns on incremental capital.
- Distinguish uncertainty that can be cheaply tested from risk that can destroy the company.
- A business should have a reason margins or economics improve with scale.
- Avoid paying indefinitely for growth that creates no durable asset.
- A $20M outcome within 36 months requires buyers or investors to believe the economics or strategic value justify the price.

## Generation questions

- What must be invested before the key assumption is known?
- What is the likely gross margin and operating leverage?
- What happens if growth slows?
- What durable asset remains after acquisition spend?
- What makes the downside survivable?
- Why would a rational acquirer pay instead of reproducing it?

## Evaluation bias

Reward:

- asymmetric economics;
- capital efficiency;
- recurring value;
- durable assets;
- credible acquisition economics.

Challenge:

- vanity growth;
- fragile margins;
- hidden working-capital needs;
- business models that consume more capital as they scale;
- valuation claims with no economic foundation.
