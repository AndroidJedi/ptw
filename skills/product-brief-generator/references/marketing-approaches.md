# Marketing approaches — policy version 1, editorial revision 6

The server supplies exactly one selected policy snapshot. Its approach is an
owner preference for this hypothesis, never proof or a universal learned rule.
Keep the raw idea's capabilities and the existing honest offer/CTA boundaries.
Return positioning in the required language: desired_identity (empty when not
useful), customer_tension, category_frame, and functional_value. These explain
one decision, not alternative campaigns. Keep the whole positioning object,
including marketing_approach, below 1024 UTF-8 bytes; prefer short sentences.
No invented capabilities, health claims, premium prices, status stereotypes,
scarcity, customer proof, or manufactured anxiety. Brand examples below explain
mechanics from an owner-supplied transcript; they are not performance evidence.

For V3, also return brand_identity: an explicit brand hypothesis, not a new
company name/logo or an essay about branding. Keep the entire object <=3072
UTF-8 bytes and each field <=260 characters. Use short, specific sentences.
This is a creative brief for subsequent copy and image agents:
- belief: the brand's point of view on a good life or good work, beyond its service.
- identity_signal: what choosing it says about the buyer; a valued self-image.
- values: two or three values made concrete through a choice or behaviour.
- cultural_tension: a real convention or frustrating tradeoff the brand challenges.
  Identity-led needs one grounded tension; benefit-led may leave it empty. Do not
  merely repeat a missing tool, money or transport.
- category_reframe: ordinary category -> meaningful world of use, not a synonym.
- emotional_reward: how the buyer feels about themselves after the choice.
- competence_cue: one useful distinction/action that makes the buyer more capable;
  teach through use rather than calling the customer smart.
- proof_anchor: supplied function that earns the emotional promise. Make the
  connection to emotional_reward clear without adding features or evidence.
- voice: a fitting manner of speaking plus a short illustrative phrase. Plain,
  precise language is valid; elevated or sensory wording is optional and must
  sound natural for the product and audience.
- visual_world: recognizable product, human action, setting and 1–2 repeatable cues
  making the values visible; scene direction is not a fleet/feature claim.
- ritual: an optional repeatable behaviour people could share, not a claim of an
  existing community, membership, merchandise or service. Empty if it adds nothing.
For benefit-led, identity_signal and emotional_reward may also be empty. Keep
brand direction practical; never force an aspirational lifestyle onto a tool.
Existing Natal identity and selected template constraints remain authoritative.
V1/V2 reservations omit brand_identity even when this reference mentions it.

## benefit_led

Lead with the customer's concrete problem, practical outcome and supported
benefits. Choose one differentiation using the existing direct-response method.
Keep the offer low-friction and honor-able. Positioning explains the customer's
tension, the ordinary useful category and the practical reason to choose this
product. An emotional benefit may support that practical promise; desired_identity
may be empty. Do not force a lifestyle or premium-category story.

## identity_led

Build one coherent strategy using four connected moves: reframe the ordinary
product in a meaningful context; name the specific convention or tradeoff it
answers; show what choosing it says about the buyer; and connect the emotional
reward to a function actually supplied by the idea. Use the examples below as
possible expressions of that strategy, not as styles to copy. Translate the
strategy into desired identity, tension, category frame and functional value.
For a sparse idea, the audience and tension are positioning hypotheses, not
claims about market research. Never invent a danger, competitor, customer shame
or product capability to create an adversary.
Privately explore distinct human occasions or aspirations, then select one
specific angle whose emotion follows from the actual function. With a sparse
idea, choose an audience and use occasion as a hypothesis; do not retreat to a
definition of the category. Do not invent supporting capabilities. The selected
angle must shape the main promise, product description, audience and offer,
not only the positioning fields. In V3 make the brand's viewpoint explicit.
A use occasion alone ("weekend with friends") is not a brand identity. Say what
that choice means, which values it expresses, and how voice/visuals embody it.
Use an ordinary category label in the functional explanation so the new context
does not obscure what the product is. Pair emotional_reward with proof_anchor;
the buyer should be able to explain the practical reason for the choice.
Specific wording can sharpen a useful distinction, but an elevated register is
only a technique when it fits; do not make a practical tool sound artisanal.
Do not add a reasoning essay or checklist outside the structured fields.

Separate hook from explanation. Aim for a 3–5-word promise, usually <=45
characters. Let product/benefits/functional_value explain how it works. Avoid
adding "without owning..." or similar service explanations to the headline.
In desired_identity name a valued role plus its underlying values: who the buyer
wants to be, not merely what they use. In category_frame make the occasion and
product's role concrete enough to guide imagery. Let them feel resourceful,
prepared or discerning through the choice and use, not "you're smarter" flattery.

Before returning, ask: could this headline describe almost every alternative
in the category? Does the identity merely say "I make good choices" or "I use
what I need"? If yes, sharpen the occasion and tension. Practical competence can
be an identity when expressed through a recognizable moment, not a generic label.
Choose a natural first action for that occasion; no automatic consultation,
discount, free trial or invented promotion.

- Touchland/Jolie, category reframing: when actual capabilities serve a broader
  customer goal, position the product in that context. A task planner can mean
  feeling prepared for the day. Packaging or health benefits absent from the
  idea cannot be invented to make the reframing work.
- Gathre, buyer tension: distinguish buyer from user when relevant. Resolve two
  legitimate desires, such as caring for a child and enjoying one's home.
  Do not infer a buyer's gender, wealth or taste from a stereotype.
- Graza, competence through use: make a supported task easier to understand
  through recognizable occasions and action language. A water-label comparison
  app can support informed shopping; it cannot diagnose water safety.
- Loop/Stanley, everyday identity: select a concrete use situation and an
  achievable self-image such as prepared, considerate, organized or confident.
  Show a believable interaction rather than generic luxury or group status.
- Starface/Welly, emotional reframing: where the idea establishes embarrassment
  or inconvenience, offer acceptance, expression or ease. Never create shame
  or anxiety so the product can appear to cure it.
- Liquid Death, category convention: name a frustrating convention such as
  jargon or tedious routines and express an honest alternative. A common enemy
  is a convention, not a fabricated danger or an attacked customer. Provocation,
  rebellion and community are optional, not universal requirements.
- Language and visible symbols: use a fitting voice, recognisable product
  interaction and recurring scene cues to express the chosen world. Elevated
  vocabulary is optional. A ritual may invite participation; never invent an
  established community or turn every product into luxury.
- Heyday, emotional appeal with a practical anchor: pair the desired feeling
  with a capability supplied by the idea. Convenience can support feeling able
  to care for oneself on a busy day; emotional language cannot replace function.

Example: for an app comparing bottled-water labels, use informed everyday choice
as identity, confusing labels as tension, a shopping helper as category, and
label comparison as functional value. A Post may say 'Know what you choose'; a
Landing can explain comparison and the approved offer. Never promise healthier
water, medical outcomes or unavailable analysis.

For "car sharing", "Gather friends for a weekend trip without owning a car"
still explains too much. An illustrative hook is "Friends. Weekend. Let's go."
or "Друзі. Вихідні. Поїхали." Identity: the resourceful friend who makes plans
happen, valuing shared experiences and choosing what the occasion needs.
Category: a car for a weekend together. Function: temporary access to a car.
The image agent can show friends loading weekend bags into a clearly readable
car, giving the vehicle purpose and the buyer initiative. These are illustrative
scene cues, not claims of a supplied fleet, accessories or booking features.
The offer can ask about a car for their outing; never promise a particular make,
luxury fleet, instant availability or free consultation. Transfer the mechanic
to other domains; do not repeat this slogan, occasion or visual universally.

For that car-sharing direction, the brand belief could be "Good weekends start
with someone saying let's go." The identity is the friend who turns the group
chat into shared memories. Values: initiative, time together, choosing for the
occasion. The tension is plans that never leave the chat, not personal inadequacy
or lack of car ownership. Reframe vehicle rental as the start of time together.
Teach a useful choice: people, bags and route first, car second; do not claim a
matching tool exists. Warm, decisive language and a recognizable car with friends
loading weekend bags express it. A proposed ritual could be sending the first
"Where this Saturday?" message. This is a hypothesis, not an existing brand fact.
Find similarly distinct beliefs for other ideas; generic "freedom", "confidence"
and a pretty picture do not constitute a brand strategy.
